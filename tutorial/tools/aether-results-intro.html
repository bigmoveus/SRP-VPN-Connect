How these tests ran

The phones were {iphone} and {android}, with {app}. Every case connected to Cloudflare WARP from our office line.

  • Each case saved a profile with the settings in its row, connected, and moved the traffic in its row through the tunnel to a test server that logged where each request came from. Every case that moved traffic checked that the requests came from a Cloudflare address (AS13335), so they left through WARP; with Tor Only, from outside Cloudflare.
  • Traffic. On the iPhone four 16 MiB transfers at a time ran for the time shown, as downloads, uploads or both. On Android each transfer case moved two 16 MiB downloads, two 16 MiB uploads, or both; the row shows how long that took.
  • Log levels. The cases named ae-log-… moved 40 seconds of downloads (10 seconds at Debug), read the connection log back and checked its lines; no level may show a key or the WARP+ license. The iPhone ran them on a Release build, the kind TestFlight ships. At Debug the check looks for the engine's and the packet bridge's lines, which only that level writes.
  • Lifecycle. The cases named ae-life-… checked On Sleep, a session that ends, a change of network and Retry Times. On Android the screen was really turned off, Wi-Fi was really switched off and on again, and the session was ended with the debug build's test command.
  • Retry Times. Its two cases need WARP out of reach. On Android Wi-Fi and mobile data were really switched off: before connecting in one case, while the VPN was up in the other. The iPhone's network cannot be switched off from outside, so there the Debug build's test switch left the search for a WARP endpoint without an answer, and the second case then ended the session with the test command.

How to read the results

LabelMeaning
VERIFIED — TESTED SUCCESSFULLYThe case did what its row expects: it connected and moved its traffic through the tunnel.
TESTED — FAILEDThe case did not do what its row expects; the row says what happened.
NOT VERIFIEDThe case did not run on that phone.

Speeds are averages over the whole transfer on our office line, to WARP's nearest edge. They show that traffic moved, and are not a benchmark. The Android phone was on office Wi-Fi with a slower uplink than the iPhone's.

Not tested in this round

  • A valid WARP+ license. The case used a key WARP does not know; binding a real one was not tried.
  • Moving between Wi-Fi and cellular on the iPhone. The iPhone's network case makes the tunnel test the session as after a change of network, without a real one.
  • Real sleep on the iPhone. The iPhone's On Sleep case tells the tunnel that the phone slept; the phone did not really sleep.
  • IPv6 inside the tunnel.
  • The iPhone without a network. The iPhone's Retry Times cases make the search for a WARP endpoint fail while the phone's network stays up; only the Android phone really lost its network.
