Configuration Reference

What the engine is, what each setting does, where the phone's traffic and names go, and what the app keeps private.

Where this page comes from

The source code of both apps and of our Aether fork, and the cases of Test results, which the descriptions below name where they show a setting at work.

The engine

Aether is a Cloudflare WARP client by CluvexStudio (github.com/CluvexStudio/Aether, AGPL 3.0). The apps build our fork of it, tag v2.4.0-srp.2 of github.com/bigmoveus/Aether: Aether 2.4.0 with these changes, each its own commit:

  • Uploads no longer stall the tunnel. Aether's network stack dropped the packets it sent whenever the queue to the MASQUE connection was full, and under an upload every connection of the tunnel backed off until nothing moved. The fork keeps them until there is room. We sent this change to Aether as CluvexStudio/Aether#168.
  • The lines that name each connection's destination are at debug level, so the engine's log names no destination at its other levels.
  • The certificate pins of the MASQUE edges include Let's Encrypt's YE2 intermediate, which signs the certificates the edges now serve.
  • The apps run the engine through its C interface: there the fork installs the logger, so the log levels apply, and lets a tunnel ask for Tor.

The settings

SettingWhat it does
The WARP identityOn its first connection the engine registers a free WARP device and keeps its keys in the app's own storage, one for MASQUE and one for WireGuard. Later connections use them again. No account or e-mail address is involved.
TransportMASQUE carries the tunnel as an HTTP CONNECT-IP request to a WARP edge on port 443, over HTTP/3 (QUIC) or HTTP/2 (TLS over TCP). With Both, the app falls back to HTTP/2 when no HTTP/3 edge works. WireGuard is WARP's original transport over UDP. Over MASQUE, the engine opens the tunnel only after a packet has gone through it; an edge that takes the request but carries no traffic is given up.
Endpoint ScanBalanced checks more of WARP's edges and takes a good one. Turbo takes the first that answers, which saves time when it works.
WARP+ LicenseAt connect time the app binds the key to the WARP device in use and checks the account type. An invalid key is refused by WARP, and the connection goes on with free WARP (case ae-warp-plus-invalid-license).
TorWARP via Tor reaches the WARP edge through Tor, over MASQUE on HTTP/2; the traffic still leaves the internet at Cloudflare. Tor Only leaves the internet at a Tor exit, without WARP. Tor needs a few seconds to half a minute more to connect: 5 to 25 seconds in our runs.
TLS FragmentationSplits the ClientHello of the HTTP/2 connection's TLS handshake, for networks that block connections by the name in it.

Routing and DNS

  • All of the phone's IPv4 traffic goes into the tunnel, with an MTU of 1400. The app's own connections to the WARP edge stay outside it.
  • The phone asks 1.1.1.1 and 1.0.0.1 for names, through the tunnel.
  • With Tor Only, the app answers name lookups itself, with stand-in addresses from 198.19.0.0/16. Each connection then carries the name into Tor, which looks it up. Tor carries TCP connections only.
  • The traffic leaves the internet with a Cloudflare address (AS13335): every case of Test results that goes through WARP checked this. With Tor Only, it leaves with a Tor exit's address.

What stays private

  • The WARP+ license is kept in the Keychain on iPhone and in the app's encrypted store on Android. No log level writes it (cases ae-warp-plus-invalid-license and the ae-*log* cases check the logs for it).
