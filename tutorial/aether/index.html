Aether

Aether connects to Cloudflare WARP, Cloudflare's free VPN service. The phone needs no server and no account. SRP VPN Connect runs the Aether engine on iPhone and Android. That engine is our fork of Aether (github.com/bigmoveus/Aether, tag v2.4.0-srp.2): Aether 2.4.0 with the app's changes (Configuration Reference).

What Aether does

The phone runs the engine. It registers a free WARP identity once and keeps it. It finds a WARP endpoint that answers and opens the tunnel to it. The phone's traffic leaves the internet at Cloudflare, with a Cloudflare address. The tunnel runs over MASQUE on HTTP/3 (QUIC over UDP), over MASQUE on HTTP/2 (TCP and TLS, where UDP is blocked), or over WARP's WireGuard. A WARP+ license and Tor are optional.

How an Aether connection works

  1. The tunnel to WARP. The engine on the phone opens it.
  2. The phone's traffic. The app takes the phone's packets and sends each connection through the engine's SOCKS5 port on the phone. Names are looked up through it as well.
  3. The test connection. The app counts as connected once a test connection through the tunnel went through. It sends one every 20 seconds, and one at once when the phone changes network. Two failed in a row mean the tunnel stopped carrying traffic, unless traffic still came back through it in the meantime. Auto Reconnect then sets up a new session, or the VPN ends with the reason.

Where to start

  1. Install the app: on iPhone from TestFlight, on Android by building it from the source; see Installation. Our tests ran the code of commit 80f12f3d on both phones; the iPhone's two Retry Times cases ran on a Debug build of eb3c0467, which adds only their test switch. On iPhone use TestFlight build 46 (commit a77a689a) or later, the first with this code; on Android, APK 1.2.0 (11) or later.
  2. Add an Aether profile and connect: Setting up the app. Nothing else is needed.
  3. Every setting of the profile, and what it does: Configuration Reference.
  4. If it does not connect: Troubleshooting.

What was tested

Test results lists every case we ran on an iPhone and on an Android phone:

  • Downloads and uploads over MASQUE on HTTP/3, on HTTP/2 and both, and over WARP's WireGuard; both directions at once; the Turbo endpoint scan; TLS Fragmentation; an invalid WARP+ license; WARP through Tor; and Tor only.
  • For the connection itself: sleep in each On Sleep mode; a session that ends, with Auto Reconnect and without; a change of network; Retry Times when WARP cannot be reached; and the log levels.
